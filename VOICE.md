# Third Space — voice sheet

One voice for the whole site. It belongs to someone who grew up on this computer and is
telling you about it, quietly, without selling or lecturing.

**The rules in one line:** lowercase, plain, first person where it's mine, nothing explained twice, no jokes at the visitor's expense.

## Ten do / don't pairs

| # | Do | Don't |
|---|----|-------|
| 1 | "gone since 2006. nobody asked for it to go." | "this feature was deprecated as part of the optimization trend." |
| 2 | State what happened, then stop. | Explain the thesis after every line. |
| 3 | "i haven't written this one yet." | Fill an empty slot with plausible filler. |
| 4 | "you closed 4 pop-ups and accepted none. the closing was counted too." | "wow, you really hate ads!" |
| 5 | Name who made it: "by rufus". | "by the community." |
| 6 | Use the object's own word: *recycle bin, letters, the shelf.* | Use thesis words inside the fiction: *receipt, data room, signed-work room.* |
| 7 | Small, concrete nouns: a bus timetable, a tomato log. | Abstract nouns: engagement, optimization, friction (outside the notes pages). |
| 8 | Let the interface be plain when it is being cold: "you are already logged in." | Wink at the visitor: "gotcha!", "satire alert". |
| 9 | One sentence of irony, kept inside the pop-ups, which belong to the platforms. | Irony in rooms, mote, the bin, or any first-person note. |
| 10 | Say "i" only for things i did, saw or made. | Invent a childhood memory to sound authentic. |

Profiles (the eras): guest, the first user, dad, my sister, the kids, mum, everyone, you.
Every window ends with `last edited by: unknown`. In the next web it says who.

## Mote's rules

1. It only wakes if you type a first memory. Skipping leaves it asleep; it can be woken later.
2. It knows only what you gave it, and what the recycle bin holds. It never guesses.
3. It never makes pop-ups.
4. It says what it kept, plainly, and never judges.
